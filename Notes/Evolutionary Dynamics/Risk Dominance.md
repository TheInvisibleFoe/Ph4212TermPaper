- **Payoff Dominance:** A strategy is payoff-dominant if it simply yields the highest possible reward if everyone coordinates on it. If $a>d$, then A is payoff-dominant. Everyone is objectively richer if everyone plays A.
- **Risk Dominance:** A strategy is risk-dominant if it is the "safer" bet when you are unsure what the other player will do. Mathematically, strategy A is risk-dominant over B if: $$a+b>c+d$$
 Risk dominance $(a+b>c+d)$ simply means that Strategy A is less risky to play in an uncertain environment, commands a larger evolutionary "target area" (basin of attraction) in infinite populations, and has a higher probability of successfully invading and replacing its rival in finite populations.

When we are playing a game, we try to play to win. Suppose we have a finite population where we do not have any information about the strategies of the other players, then to win by playing strat A, We ask the question,
> **Whether A is more likely to replace B or vice versa.**

Mathematically, this corresponds to saying that, in the weak selection limit
$$
	\rho_{A} > \rho_{B} \implies (N-2)(a-d)>N(c-b)
$$
In the limit of large population we obtain the condition,
$$
	a+b>c+d \implies x^* < \frac{1}{2}
$$
